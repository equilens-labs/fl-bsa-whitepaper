\section{Methods}

\subsection{Single-run fairness inference}

For each observed group count, the paper uses a two-sided 95\% Wilson score interval
($\alpha=0.05$) without a continuity correction \citep{brown2001interval}. For AIR, the producer applies a delta-method
interval on the log ratio. With estimated rates $\widehat p_g$ and $\widehat p_r$, the approximate
variance is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\!\left[\log(\widehat p_g/\widehat p_r)\right]
  = \frac{1-\widehat p_g}{n_g\widehat p_g}
  + \frac{1-\widehat p_r}{n_r\widehat p_r}.
  \label{eq:air-delta}
\end{equation}
The log-scale interval is exponentiated. SRG uses the conservative endpoint range in
\autoref{eq:srg-interval}; it is not labelled a 95\% confidence interval. Two-sided p-values use a
two-proportion $z$ test. The four race comparisons apply Holm--Bonferroni adjustment to the AIR
p-values; unadjusted values remain in the evidence. The displayed race AIR intervals are pairwise
95\% intervals and are not multiplicity-adjusted, so an interval may exclude one even when the
corresponding Holm-adjusted p-value does not reject.

All displayed AIR intervals and SRG endpoint ranges come from
\path{intake/metrics_uncertainty.json} and \path{intake/fairness_slices.json}. The bootstrap fields
retained in the manifest and
\path{intake/metrics_long.csv} are a compatibility annex; they are not the headline interval
source. This separation matters because the two surfaces use different interval constructions.

\subsection{What the intervals and p-values mean}

The inference treats the generated rows as the observed dataset. The historical slice contains
3,000 fixture rows; each generated branch contains 10,000 rows because that was the configured
release-evidence output size. Ten thousand is a scenario choice, not a naturally occurring sample
size. It narrows conditional intervals and contributes to the amplification branch's very small
p-value. The p-value therefore says that equal rates are incompatible with this generated table
under the test model; it does not measure substantive importance or the probability that a lender
discriminated.

The single-run intervals exclude uncertainty from fixture construction, generator fitting,
scenario selection, hyperparameter choice, protected-attribute encoding, unobserved groups,
deployment drift, and variation across generation seeds. The paper reports them because they
reproduce the release evidence exactly, but does not relabel them as total uncertainty.

The intrinsic row is a different object. Its approvals are allocated by the configured equal-rate
post-label policy, subject only to integer allocation across protected groups. Applying the same
AIR interval and two-proportion formulas to those policy-determined counts produces the displayed
range and $p$-value, but neither measures uncertainty in the policy output. They are retained only
for format symmetry with the historical and amplification rows and have no inferential
interpretation.

\subsection{Branch-quality characterization}

Each branch certificate reports an overall score, an aggregate distribution score, correlation
preservation, demographic-proportion drift, and a privacy heuristic. The overall gate threshold is
0.80. Scores are reported for both branches; neither branch substitutes for the other. The privacy
number is explicitly scoped as a quality heuristic. The release claims no differential-privacy
budget, and near-duplicate detection was not computed. The exact-output wall reports zero exact
training-row reproductions before and after its final check in this run, a much narrower fact than
a general privacy guarantee.

The producer certificate's downstream-utility field is \texttt{not\_evaluated} for both branches.
The separate study below fills part of that evidence gap without retroactively changing the
certificate.

\subsection{Multi-seed Gold characterization}

The exact release includes ten planned and completed seeds across four scenarios: balanced,
gender-bias positive control, outliers, and security. The companion preserves the exact merged
summary, requirements, disposition, evidence manifest, and 40-row index. We report the minimum,
mean, and maximum scenario disparity ratio and the gate outcome across those runs. These are
descriptive seed bands; ten fixed seeds do not define a random-effects population or a confidence
interval for future deployments.

\subsection{Synthetic-fixture utility study}

The companion adds a reproducible train-on-synthetic/test-on-held-out-original (TSTR) study. It
uses the exact generated balanced fixture from the v5.0.1 Gold artifact. A deterministic stratified
split assigns 3,500 rows to training and 1,500 to testing. The downstream classifier is median
imputation, standardization, and logistic regression over age, income, credit score, loan amount,
and debt-to-income. Gender and race are excluded. The real-train baseline and each synthetic-train
model are evaluated on the same held-out fixture.

The amplification generator is fit once on the 3,500-row training partition. It then produces ten
training tables, one for each Gold seed, each with exactly 3,500 rows so row-count expansion does
not advantage the synthetic models. ROC AUC, average precision, accuracy at 0.5, Brier score, and
log loss are recorded. This design measures generation-seed variation conditional on one fixture,
split, generator fit, and classifier family. It excludes fixture-generation uncertainty,
generator-refit uncertainty, customer distribution shift, and the intrinsic branch because that
branch changes the outcome through its post-label policy.

For a portable AUC comparison, skill-normalised retention is
$(\operatorname{AUC}_{synthetic}-0.5)/(\operatorname{AUC}_{baseline}-0.5)$. Zero means no retained
skill above chance, one matches the real-train fixture baseline, and a negative value is worse than
chance. The raw ratio $\operatorname{AUC}_{synthetic}/\operatorname{AUC}_{baseline}$ is not reported
because it assigns positive ``retention'' to chance and worse-than-chance classifiers.

\subsection{Determinism and derivation}

Derived JSON, TeX tables, and figures are generated from checked-in machine-readable inputs. The
build fixes timezone and \texttt{SOURCE\_DATE\_EPOCH}, suppresses volatile PDF engine fields, and
uses fixed PDF metadata for Matplotlib outputs. The companion builder sorts member paths, fixes ZIP
timestamps and permissions, hashes each member, and then runs an offline verifier. Determinism
demonstrates repeatable transformation of fixed inputs; it does not make the inputs representative.
