\section{Results}

\subsection{Gender selection-rate characterization}

Table~\ref{tab:characterization-slices} separates the point-screen relation from the interval-screen
relation. The historical fixture AIR is \HistoricalAIR{}. Its interval crosses 0.80, so the point
is below the internal line while the conditional interval is not wholly below it. Amplification AIR
is \AmplificationAIR{} with interval [\AmplificationAIRLower{}, \AmplificationAIRUpper{}], entirely
below the line. The difference between the historical and amplification points is about
$8.0\times10^{-5}$, which is consistent with signal preservation for this fixed run.

The intrinsic parity-policy control has AIR \IntrinsicAIR{}. Its approvals are policy-determined

not show that FL-BSA discovered or repaired the cause of the historical gap.

The inferential two-sided p-values are approximately $2.54\times10^{-14}$ for the 3,000-row
historical slice and $5.93\times10^{-44}$ for the 10,000-row amplification slice. The latter's
extreme significance is partly a consequence of generating 10,000 rows. Effect size, interval,
sample construction, and scenario purpose are therefore reported alongside the test. The
intrinsic value above is not included among these inferential results.

\subsection{Race method and bounded result}

White is the configured provenance reference. The active highest-rate policy selected black as
the effective reference at selection rate 0.5411. The worst pair is other versus black, AIR
0.8818 with conditional interval [0.7814, 0.9950]. Its unadjusted two-sided p-value is 0.0405 and
its Holm-adjusted AIR p-value is 0.1618. Hispanic has the smallest count, 273. These values are
disclosed for method clarity but are not promoted to a headline fairness verdict. The pairwise AIR
interval is not multiplicity-adjusted, whereas the reported p-value is Holm-adjusted; this is why
the interval can exclude one while the adjusted test does not reject.
The complete machine-readable intake is in \path{intake/selection_rates.csv} and
\path{intake/metrics_uncertainty.json}, both included in the companion.

\subsection{Branch quality}

Both branch certificates exceed their configured overall-quality threshold: amplification scores
\AmplificationQuality{} and intrinsic scores \IntrinsicQuality{}. The component view is more
informative than either total. Amplification distribution score is 0.999 and correlation
preservation is 0.961; intrinsic values are 0.954 and 0.978. Maximum demographic-proportion drift
is 0.0067 percentage points for amplification and 0.0567 percentage points for intrinsic.

\subsection{Utility evidence}

The real-train fixture baseline ROC AUC is \UtilityBaselineAUC{}. Across ten equal-size
synthetic-training tables, mean ROC AUC is \UtilitySyntheticAUCMean{}, ranging from
\UtilitySyntheticAUCMin{} to \UtilitySyntheticAUCMax{}. Mean skill-normalised AUC retention is
\UtilitySkillRetentionMean{} (\UtilitySkillRetentionMeanPct{}\%), with range
\UtilitySkillRetentionMin{}--\UtilitySkillRetentionMax{}. Negative values are worse than chance;
one would match the real-train baseline. The mean synthetic-train AUC is approximately chance, so
this study does not establish train-on-synthetic predictive utility. Average precision and log
loss show the same general limitation.

\subsection{Explicit non-results}

ECE remains unevaluated because no probability-score surface exists. EO remains non-informative
because its confusion cells are degenerate. No conclusion is drawn for unencoded protected bases,
intersectional combinations, customer portfolios, causal effects, production drift, or legal
compliance.
