
\section{Compliance Mapping}
This section supplies current interpretation boundaries, not a compliance conclusion. Applicability depends on the institution, role, jurisdiction, product, use, and effective date; legal and compliance owners must review the deployment facts.

The 2026 Regulation B final rule, effective 21 July 2026, states that ECOA does not authorize disparate-impact liability or an effects test \citep{cfpb_regb_2026}. AIR is therefore used in this paper only as a voluntarily selected internal fairness screen and descriptive monitoring ratio. It is not presented as a Regulation B requirement, an ECOA disparate-impact test, or a lending-law verdict. Other statutory, constitutional, contractual, state, and jurisdictional questions can still require separate review.

For FCA Consumer Duty, generated group-level diagnostics and provenance can support a qualified owner's review, but a synthetic fixture does not demonstrate good customer outcomes or FCA compliance \citep{fca_consumer_duty_2026}. Likewise, model-purpose, limitations, monitoring, provenance, and evidence-chain records may support case-specific SR 26-2 or \EUAIAct{} governance work \citep{sr26_2,eu_ai_act}; this paper does not determine applicability, conformance, certification, or supervisory acceptance.

Appendix~D preserves every row of the producer-supplied run-time mapping for provenance. Its requirement, control, owner, and status cells are source metadata, not adopted legal validation or compliance findings. This dated release interpretation supersedes that producer narrative for the paper's claims without mutating the exact intake.
