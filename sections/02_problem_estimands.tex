\section{Audit Questions and Estimands}

\subsection{Decision surface}

Let $A$ be a declared protected attribute, $g$ one of its observed groups, and
$\widehat{Y}\in\{0,1\}$ the observed decision label in the characterization data. For each group,
the selection rate is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_g = \frac{y_g}{n_g},
  \label{eq:selection-rate}
\end{equation}
where $y_g$ is the number of selected rows and $n_g$ is the number of eligible rows in the group.
These are row-level fixture summaries, not estimates of applicant eligibility, creditworthiness, or
causal treatment effects.

\subsection{Disparity ratio}

For a comparison group $g$ and reference group $r$, the paper reports
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{AIR}(g,r) = \frac{\widehat p_g}{\widehat p_r}.
  \label{eq:air}
\end{equation}
The label ``AIR'' is retained for continuity with the machine-readable evidence. It is a descriptive
selection-rate ratio. A value of one means equal observed rates; it does not by itself identify
fairness, discrimination, harm, causation, or legal compliance.

For binary gender, the configured comparison is female versus male. Race is multi-group. The
publication evidence keeps white as the configured reference in provenance, but its active
pairwise screen selects the group with the highest observed rate. Black has the highest rate in
this run and is therefore the effective reference. The worst observed pair is other versus black.
The separate configured-reference helper still present in the product source is not used to create
the headline race uncertainty object; the coexistence of those two policies remains a product-side
consistency issue described in \autoref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Selection-rate gap}

The signed selection-rate gap is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{SRG}(g,r) = \widehat p_g - \widehat p_r.
  \label{eq:srg}
\end{equation}
Its conservative endpoint-difference range is
\begin{equation}
  [L_g-U_r,\; U_g-L_r],
  \label{eq:srg-interval}
\end{equation}
where $[L_g,U_g]$ and $[L_r,U_r]$ are separate 95\% Wilson score intervals. The exact machine
value is \path{conservative_wilson_endpoint_difference}. Although its inputs are 95\% intervals,
the resulting endpoint-difference range is not itself a calibrated 95\% confidence interval. It is
also not the classical Newcombe interval; the numeric endpoints themselves were not changed by
correcting the method label.

\subsection{Internal screen and interval relation}

The statistical analysis plan chooses 0.80 as an internal AIR screen. The point relation is either
``below'' or ``at/above.'' Independently, the confidence interval can be entirely below, cross, or
be entirely above the line. Reporting both prevents a point estimate from silently becoming a
binary compliance label.

The 0.80 convention originates in US employment-selection guidance. The EEOC describes the
four-fifths convention as a rule of thumb rather than a legal definition and notes that it is not
controlling in all circumstances \citep{eeoc_uniform_guidelines}. It is not presented here as a
lending requirement.

\subsection{Unevaluated and non-informative surfaces}

Expected calibration error requires probability scores and calibration bins. Neither exists for
this decision-label fixture, so ECE is \texttt{NOT\_EVALUATED}; no calibration conclusion follows.
Equalized-odds summaries were requested, but the generated confusion surface contains degenerate
cells by construction. EO is therefore \texttt{NOT\_INFORMATIVE}, not a pass. These explicit states
are preferable to imputing zeros or treating missing evidence as favorable evidence.
