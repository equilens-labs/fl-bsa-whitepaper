that the underlying feature distribution is unbiased or that an equal approval rate is an
appropriate production policy.

\subsection{Race display policy}
\label{sec:pair-selection}

The effective pairwise race comparison uses the group with the highest observed selection rate as
reference for the run; that is Black, even though the configuration's provenance reference is
White. The worst retained pair (not an automated headline verdict) is Other versus Black, AIR \RaceWorstAIR{} with 95\% interval
[\RaceWorstAIRLCI{}, \RaceWorstAIRUCI{}] and Holm--Bonferroni adjusted p-value
\RaceWorstAIRPValueAdj{}. Because the Hispanic group has only \RaceObservedMinGroupN{} rows
(2.73\%), the predeclared 300-row and 5\% display conditions are not met. The race calculation is
retained in the companion and must not be read as an unconditional race verdict. The reference and
pair were selected from the observed rates before the four emitted pairwise tests. Holm adjustment
covers that emitted family but not the data-dependent comparator selection; the interval and
p-value are descriptive and exploratory, not confirmatory post-selection inference.

\textit{*Intrinsic demographic-rate fidelity defaults to 1.000 because its certificate comparison
surface has no outcome; it is not an observed perfect result. The producer-defined composite
includes this default.}

Both composites exceed 0.80. Amplification's marginal-distribution and demographic-rate-fidelity
scores are close to one for this fixture. The side-by-side composites have different evaluated
surfaces and are not an apples-to-apples branch ranking: amplification is scored after deterministic,
outcome-stratified quantile alignment against the original input, while intrinsic has no recorded
quantile-alignment stage and its distribution component compares only the seven feature/protected
columns before post-labeling. Intrinsic does not score the later outcome, and its demographic-rate
component is the default described above. These scores characterize the exact synthetic run and do
not generalize to customer data without a new deployment-specific evaluation.
